Figure 2. Tissue context and intervention timing

Three mechanistic studies illustrate information relevant to the use of AI virtual cells in repair. (A) The epidermal–dermal model links wound geometry, fibrin and generic interface signals to epithelial closure and scar organisation [91]. The inset shows the healthy/keloid fibroblast migration model [166]. (B) A renal cell-network model guided Wnt1 pretreatment before ischaemic injury followed by ICG-001, with repair and fibrosis assessed in mice [93]. (C) A pulmonary spring-network model relates stretch and stiffness signalling to healing-like and fibrotic structures [92]. Solid arrows indicate specified relationships or intervention order; dashed arrows indicate predictions.

Alt text: Skin geometry, a sequential renal Wnt regimen and pulmonary stretch–stiffness feedback illustrate tissue context and intervention timing.
